# Small style reference set

These are reusable editorial techniques, not factual sources or mandatory
article structures. Select at most two that serve the current reader's
question. Private approved examples may accompany a user-supplied profile;
their facts, wording, and approval scope do not transfer to a new article.

| Technique | Use when | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Development, then explanation | A specific event creates the reader's question; define an unfamiliar concept when it explains the consequence | Do not add a current-status claim or concrete scene without evidence |
| Case, mechanism, return to case | A documented action becomes clearer after explaining the process behind it | Do not invent a person, interview, reaction, or motive |
| Follow-up that resolves a condition | The previous paragraph leaves a genuine limitation or mechanism unresolved | Do not turn every transition into a question or let an analogy replace the explanation |
| Choice and consequence | A sourced decision gives the reader a concrete way into a broader issue | Do not fabricate an anecdote or treat one case as a representative sample |
| Follow the transaction | The central misunderstanding concerns who acts, receives money, bears a cost, or assumes a risk | Preserve time periods, units, attribution, and contractual or legal qualifications |
| Development, practical use, concept | A practical use makes an unfamiliar concept easier to understand | Familiarity is not legal equivalence; keep expert views attributed |

For an approved edit pair retain channel, date and approval scope; a short
original and edit; the underlying problem; the reusable action; and where it
does not apply. Keep private examples in the private profile. Learn the
function of a passage rather than copying its phrasing.
