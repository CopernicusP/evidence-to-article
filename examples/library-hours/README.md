# A synthetic bilingual example

All material in this directory is invented for demonstrating the workflow. It does not describe a real library, user experience or completed trial.

## Source packet S1

- A library has approved a four-week trial.
- Friday closing will move from 6 p.m. to 8 p.m.
- The trial has not begun.
- The number of seats will remain unchanged.
- Demand will be evaluated after the trial.
- No crowding measurements or reader interviews are provided.

## Brief

Audience: readers interested in using the library in the evening. Explain what the extra opening time changes and what it cannot yet tell us. Use grounded personal analysis. Compose the languages independently from S1. These short examples demonstrate an opening and argument, not full-length client deliverables or a completed audit run.

## Compare the approach

A flat summary might say: “The extended operating hours improve service availability. The trial arrangement and capacity conditions should be considered together.”

The [Chinese example](article-zh.md) starts with the additional time, then asks what remains unknown about seats. The [English example](article-en.md) develops the same distinction with its own paragraph order. Their first-person lines express a choice of what to examine; they do not invent a visit or claim an observed outcome.

No automatic readability score or fact-checking certification is attached. Use a new source packet to evaluate whether the method transfers.
